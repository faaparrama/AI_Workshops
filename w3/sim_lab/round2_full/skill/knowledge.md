# Consequence Detective — knowledge.md

## Source

Eggen, P., & Kauchak, D. (2025). *Educational psychology: Windows on classrooms* (12th ed.). Pearson. Chapter on behaviorism (operant conditioning).

## Ideas, in order

Each one is a sentence a learner can get wrong.

1.  A consequence is defined by what happens to the behavior next, not by what the adult meant.
2.  Reinforcement makes a behavior go **up**. Punishment makes it go **down**.
3.  "Positive" means something is **added**. "Negative" means something is **taken away**. Neither word means good or bad.
4.  Negative reinforcement takes away something unpleasant, so the behavior goes up.
5.  Presentation punishment adds something unpleasant. Removal punishment takes away something the learner wants.
6.  A teacher can punish by accident: if a behavior drops after a teacher's reaction, that reaction worked as punishment.
