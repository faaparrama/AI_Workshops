# Where Does Confidence Come From? — scenarios.md

Optional. A tool with fixed questions keeps them here, so the agent never writes its own.
Eight scenes, in this order. The answer and a one-line "why" follow each.

1. **Rosa** missed every free throw in October. She practiced each day after school. Now she makes six of ten, and she asks to shoot in the game.
   Answer: Mastery experience.
   Why: She did it herself, after real effort.

2. **Theo** thinks the science fair is for "smart kids." He watches his friend from the same reading group win a ribbon. Theo signs up.
   Answer: Vicarious experience.
   Why: Someone like him succeeded, and he watched.

3. **Ana** freezes before her speech. Her coach says, "You've got this. I've heard you practice." Ana walks to the front.
   Answer: Verbal persuasion.
   Why: Someone she trusts told her she can.

4. **Eli** feels his stomach drop when the math test lands on his desk. He decides he isn't ready and skips the hard problems.
   Answer: Physiological and emotional state.
   Why: He read his own body as a sign he would fail.

5. **Priya** fixed her bike chain by herself last week. Today the chain slips again. She grabs the tools without asking anyone.
   Answer: Mastery experience.
   Why: She already did this task once, herself.

6. **Omar** has never sung in front of anyone. His teacher shows a video of a student from his own school, nervous, singing at last year's show. Omar signs up for choir.
   Answer: Vicarious experience.
   Why: He watched someone like him do it.

7. **Lily** reads her poem aloud. Her hands stop shaking by the second line, and her voice steadies. She reads the next poem without notes.
   Answer: Physiological and emotional state.
   Why: She read her calmer body as "I can do this."

8. **Sam** gets a note from his grandmother: "You always figure things out." He tries the puzzle one more time.
   Answer: Verbal persuasion.
   Why: Someone he trusts told him he can.
