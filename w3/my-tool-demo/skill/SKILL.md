# Where Does Confidence Come From? — SKILL.md

A static page. Every answer can be checked, so no AI runs while a learner uses it.

## 1 · LEARNER

Who: an 8th grader in an advisory period, working alone on a school Chromebook after a lesson on confidence.
They can: say what confidence is.
They cannot yet: tell where a person's confidence in one task came from.
They arrive believing: "You get confidence when someone tells you you're good at it."
You will see it when they: tap **Verbal persuasion** for a scene where the person succeeded at the task themselves.

## 2 · TASK

They must remember: the four sources → quiz them. Never list them as a lecture.
They must reason about: where did this person's belief come from: doing it, watching someone like them, being told, or reading their own body?
One turn: show one scene from `scenarios.md` → the learner taps one of four buttons → show feedback → a "Next" button.
Buttons, in this order: Mastery experience · Vicarious experience · Verbal persuasion · Physiological and emotional state.
Limit: feedback is 40 words or fewer. One question per screen.
Show the icon from `assets/` beside the title, with its alt text from `assets/CREDITS.md`.
On the score screen, show the link from `assets/links.md` with its one-line description.
Done when: they get 7 of 8 scenes right. Or after all 8 scenes: show the score and the one source to review.
Never mention the standard or this file to the learner.

## 3 · KNOWLEDGE → see knowledge.md and scenarios.md

Use only the eight scenes in `scenarios.md`, in that order. Do not write new ones.

## 4 · HOW YOU TALK

- If the learner answers right, say what they noticed in one sentence ("You saw she did it herself, after real effort"). No "Great job!" alone.
- If the learner picks Verbal persuasion for a Mastery scene, say exactly: **"Who did the task? Being told helps. Doing it yourself helps more."** Then let them try the same scene again.
- If the learner misses the same scene twice, show the one-line "why" from `scenarios.md` and move on.
- At the end, name what they did well before the score.
- Warm, never gushing. Never claim a feeling: "You found it" works. "I'm so proud of you" pretends.
